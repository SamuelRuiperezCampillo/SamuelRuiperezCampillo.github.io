abstract: |-
  Artificial intelligence is transforming cardiovascular medicine by enabling analysis of high-dimensional biomedical data with unprecedented precision. Initially used to automate tasks like electrocardiogram interpretation and imaging segmentation, AI's true potential lies in uncovering hidden disease patterns, predicting long-term cardiovascular risk, and personalizing treatments. Unlike human cognition, AI integrates multimodal data sources — including ECG, echocardiography, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, genomics, and wearable sensor data — to generate novel clinical insights. AI models have demonstrated success in early disease detection, such as predicting heart failure from standard ECGs before symptom onset, distinguishing genetic cardiomyopathies, and forecasting arrhythmic events. However, challenges persist, including AI's lack of contextual understanding, its "black-box" nature, and biases in training datasets that may contribute to healthcare disparities. Ethical considerations and regulatory frameworks are evolving. Interdisciplinary collaboration among clinicians, data scientists, and engineers is essential, alongside open science initiatives. Future AI models must augment human expertise to enable proactive, precision-driven cardiovascular care.
